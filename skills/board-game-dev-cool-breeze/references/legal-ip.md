# Legal and intellectual property

This is engineering practice, not legal advice. When in doubt, the owner decides with proper
advice.

The line the method keeps: game mechanics may be implemented in original code; expressive material
may not be copied. That means an original name and product identity, original art and iconography,
original card frames and layout, and rules explained in your own words. Do not recreate a
published game's look, characters, art, logo or text, even renamed. When the owner asks for a
specific published game, offer an original game in its genre with similar mechanics, a licensed
adaptation, or a public-domain game, and agree the new identity at kickoff.

Keep `docs/legal.md` with the unofficial-project notice, the provenance and licence of every
font, sound and data source, and the policy for trademark mentions and takedown requests.

Themes may parody, pastiche or deconstruct anything the owner likes; the art direction has no
content limits beyond originality and the law.

## Lessons

Each lesson is a rule and its reason. The label says how strong the evidence was: *seen once* (one concrete finding), *seen repeatedly* (recurring failures or independent checks across subsystems), or *owner decision* (an accepted preference, which a new owner may change).

- **11.01** Implement behavior with original code, original visual assets and original explanatory wording. Reusing a game's mechanics does not provide permission to copy its expressive materials. *(seen repeatedly)*

- **11.02** Keep source provenance and licenses for code, fonts, sounds, data references and artwork separately. A code license does not automatically cover every file in a mixed-media repository. *(seen repeatedly)*

- **11.03** Read implementations to understand behavior only within their licensing constraints, and credit permitted reuse. Public availability and a repository license badge do not establish permission for every embedded asset. *(seen repeatedly)*

- **11.04** Keep downloaded rulebooks, source photos and private extraction material outside the public repository. Publish independently expressed facts and rulings rather than the reference documents themselves. *(seen repeatedly)*

- **11.05** Review rules prose for inherited wording as well as completeness, using phrase checks as a flag for human inspection. Passing a phrase matcher does not by itself establish legal clearance. *(seen repeatedly)*

- **11.06** Draw an original icon system, frames and product identity instead of tracing official visual trade dress. Merely regenerating a familiar official composition does not make the result original. *(seen repeatedly)*

- **11.07** Use a clear unofficial-project notice and a deliberate policy for trademark references, monetization and takedown requests. These are project governance choices to confirm, not a universal legal safe harbor. *(seen repeatedly)*

- **11.08** Preserve font source and license files and check naming restrictions when subsetting or outlining. A locally installed attractive font is not evidence that the project can redistribute it. *(seen repeatedly)*

- **11.09** Audit a public push for personal paths, secrets, identities and infrastructure details, then inspect matches in context. Crude secret and hostname patterns produced false positives as well as useful catches. *(seen repeatedly)*

- **11.10** License every public repository explicitly, code and assets separately where they differ, and remove operator-specific information from instructions and examples before publishing. *(owner decision)*
